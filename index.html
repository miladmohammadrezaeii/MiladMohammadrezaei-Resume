  • Thesis: Physical Features Extraction for Online SoH Estimation of Li-ion Batteries (Data-Driven)
  • Supervisors: Dr. A. Tabesh, Dr. Z. Maleki • Advisor: Dr. S. A. Khajehoddin

The expansion of electrical energy storage technologies has gained significant attention over the past two decades due to their fundamental role in enabling the large-scale integration of renewable energy sources, enhancing the resilience of power grids against natural disasters and cyberattacks, and supporting the transition to electric and hybrid transportation systems. In recent years, high-capacity lithium-ion batteries have attracted particular interest in applications such as electric vehicles and microgrids, owing to their competitive advantages including high energy and power density as well as long cycle life. Optimal utilization of these batteries throughout their service life, whether through redeployment in secondary applications or timely replacement before critical failures occur, requires accurate state-of-health (SOH) estimation and reliable prediction of the remaining useful life (RUL). Methods employed for SOH estimation must achieve sufficient accuracy, as premature replacement of high-capacity batteries is not economically feasible due to their high capital cost, while delayed replacement may result in severe consequences for the control and stability of storage-integrated microgrids. This dissertation proposes a data-driven framework for SOH diagnosis of lithium-ion batteries, enabling online prediction of battery health across different sizes and capacities by extracting normalized, dimensionless features. The primary innovation of the proposed method lies in defining and extracting such normalized features, which serve as universal inputs for data-driven models and are particularly valuable given the challenges associated with acquiring suitable datasets for high-capacity batteries. In addition, an analytical SOH estimation approach based on an electrochemical model is presented, where model parameters are iteratively updated using online measurements of battery voltage and current through a least-squares minimization scheme. Unlike conventional approaches, both methods developed in this dissertation are designed for online implementation and maintain accuracy under partial charge–discharge cycling conditions. The proposed methodologies exploit shared electrochemical properties of lithium-ion batteries to reduce the influence of capacity and size on extracted features, thereby enabling the development of a generalized online SOH estimation model transferable across various lithium-ion battery types. Validation with benchmark datasets from NASA, the University of Maryland, and the University of Oxford demonstrates that the proposed method achieves a mean error of approximately 3.5% in lifetime prediction, representing a substantial improvement over existing methods with errors exceeding 13%.

Publications

A Correlation-based Method for the Estimation of the SoH of Lithium Batteries in EVs
Energy Engineering and Management
2025
Authors: M. Mohammadrezaei, Z. Maleki, A. Tabesh

Correlation-based, data-driven SoH estimation tailored to EV usage.

peer-reviewed

This paper presents a correlation-based framework for feature selection to estimate the lithium batteries state of health (SoH) using data-driven methods. These features can be calculated online for partial charge/discharge (C/D) cycles in electric vehicles. The main contribution is to provide a framework to determine a subset of independent features with maximum impacts on SoH estimation. Despite statistical features, the selected features are independent of C/D profiles, without requiring a full cycle information. Using the correlation criterion reduces the computational burden in a data-driven model as required in an online battery health estimation method. Extracting features, considering battery electrochemical reactions, and reducing the number of selected features, using the suggested correlation criterion, increase the robustness of the model and avoid overfitting. NASA labeled battery aging dataset is used to evaluate the performance of the method under various partial and random C/D cycles. The results show that the correlation-based features in Gaussian data-driven model can estimate the battery SoH with an average error of 10% under random patterns in a partial C/D cycle.

A Framework for Normalizing Physical Features of Li-ion Batteries
IEEE Transactions on Transportation Electrification
2024
Authors: M. Mohammadrezaei, Z. Maleki, A. Tabesh, S. A. Khajehoddin

Feature normalization enabling generic SoH models across cells/packs.

peer-reviewed

This article presents a framework for health estimation of a Li-ion battery that is conceptually formed based on dimensionless and common physical characteristics of a Li-ion battery. The proposed normalized features and framework enable the formation of a data-driven model to be trained using an available dataset and applied to estimate the state of health (SoH) of another Li-ion battery. It is established based on introducing the new concepts of participation factors and normalized dimensionless features which are independent of a battery capacity and its dimensions. Effective training of a model using a conventional data-driven approach needs an enriched dataset that is not readily available, particularly in high-power and custom-made battery energy storage applications. The reason is that data generation needs time-consuming and expensive lifespan experiments using several samples of Li-ion batteries. To evaluate the performance of the proposed framework, four widely used Li-ion battery datasets with different capacities and dimensions are selected and compared with conventional z -score and min-max normalization methods. Test results show that the average error using the proposed method in the early stage of battery lifespan is 3.5% that is 10% less than the error in existing methods.
